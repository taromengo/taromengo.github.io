Product record platform

Used by regional teams in APAC

Finding and comparing product records now takes 5–15 minutes instead of an hour.

Produced end to end, from stakeholder interviews to the database and the front end.

The problem

Product and evaluated-sample records were spread across 50 Excel datasets. People could not easily tell which file held what they needed.

Comparing records across projects, regions and time was harder still. A typical lookup and comparison took an estimated hour.

What I owned

I led the project end to end. I found and interviewed the stakeholders, designed the schema, built the ingestion and the migration from Excel, set up database access, and built the app people use.

How it works

Ingest and clean

Data from the Excel files lands first in staging tables. There it is cleaned and checked for consistency before it moves into the main database. I also wrote agent skills: reusable instructions that let an LLM agent, running on a commercial model through its API, automate parts of the cleaning.

One relational schema

The records live in a PostgreSQL schema of 13 tables. It uses one-to-many, many-to-one and many-to-many relationships, so a record can be compared across projects, regions and time instead of across files.

Controlled access

Access goes through controlled PostgreSQL user accounts, and the app pools its connections through SQLAlchemy.

Search without SQL

A search app lets non-technical users filter records, compare them and download the raw data, without writing SQL. A comparison page follows items over time, and a dashboard shows stakeholders which categories or markets are short of data.

Decisions

Started with stakeholder interviews

I identified the people who worked with these records and garnered user stories through interviews. From there, I designed the platform to address two pain points: not knowing which file holds what, and not being able to easily compare across projects, regions and time.

Staged the data before migrating it

Instead of loading the spreadsheets straight into the final tables, I passed them through staging tables with cleaning and consistency checks, then migrated them to ensure dirty data is out of production.

Trade-off. New data takes one extra step before people can use it.

An app in front of the database

The users are non-technical, so they get an app rather than SQL access, and a front end makes the records easy to browse. Behind it, controlled accounts and pooled connections manage database access.

Evidence

A typical task of finding records and comparing them went from an estimated one hour to 5–15 minutes. 50 Excel datasets now sit in one shared database.
